<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
When summer rises—
the first breath of youth—
it is a welcoming warmth,
slow and stretching,
morning's hand tracing
your soft baby skin,
turning golden
at the touch.
Then—
the warmth swells into heat,
crawling beneath flushed, restless skin.
You linger.
Heart pounding.
Breath caught.
Chasing years that slip like sweat.
The sun hangs high—
its shadow stretches,
but never slows.
The heat
becomes a blaze.
Sizzling your fingerprints.
Torching your rough skin.
You shut your eyes.
Time burns away.
All that's left is fire.
Then—
silence.
As quickly as it rose,
summer falls,
leaving you molten inside.
When the ash cools,
what remains
is the pearly skeleton of time—
the shape of the life you've grown into,
pale as dawn's first light.
</div>
