*Written in November 2025.*

These four poems approach a similar question through different scenes: how does the world leave its mark on things and people without necessarily noticing them? Rain, frost, a crowd, and a river are all forces that move or change what they touch, yet none of them acts with human intention. The poems give these forces the language of memory, speech, and feeling while returning to silence, anonymity, and endurance.

The sequence begins with the quiet intimacy of rain and frost, moves into the loneliness of a crowded station, and ends with a stone gradually worn down by water. Across these settings, recurring images of water, cold, movement, and stillness connect what is outwardly happening to what remains unspoken.

### Rain

<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
The sky has learned to hold its tongue.
Not thunder—just a silver thumb
pressed to the world's exhausted brow,
a touch that asks for nothing now.
The hedges drink without a sound.
The garden sinks. On holy ground
of window ledge and rusted nail,
small signatures of water trail.
One streak, then two—they choose their slope
like memory learning how to cope.
The eaves let fall their counted beads—
each one a syllable that bleeds
into the dirt, unnamed, unheard,
more prayer than any spoken word.
And somewhere in the house, a hand
uncurls against the window's band
of cool and quiet. Nothing's said.
The rain just shines upon the dead
and living both, and does not claim
the salt that gives the drops their name.
</div>

### Frost

<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
The grass has learned a kind of pause.
No wind—just teeth of silver cause
the ground to stiffen, blade by blade,
as if the earth were afraid to fade.

The pump sits still. The gate won’t swing.
On every rusted, fragile thing—
a stalk, a stone, a broken plow—
the cold lays down its careful vow.

One crystal, then a lacework spreads
where spider silk once braided threads.
The morning bends its muted light
through feathers of the lost and slight.

And in the barn, a breath of steam
unfolds like a half-remembered dream.
The horse’s flank, the frosted pail—
both hold a silence, sharp and frail.

No crow calls out. No tractor starts.
The frost just settles on the hearts
of furrow and of fur, the same—
a tender, wordless, burning flame.
</div>

### The Crowd

<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
The station has forgotten your name.
No malice—just the squeal of train
on track, the shuffle of a thousand feet
that never pause, never meet.

The voices blur to one grey sound.
A child's balloon is loosely wound
around a stranger's fingertip—
then gone. You watch the mother's lip

form words that aren't for you. The screen
flicks red, then blue, then in-between.
Each face a door that will not knock,
each back a wall, each tick of clock

a tiny severance. Someone laughs
near your left ear. The escalator drafts
carry the scent of someone's rain-soaked coat,
a wedding ring, a crumpled note

that falls. No one bends down but you.
By the time you stand, the crowd has grew
a thicker skin. You hold the paper—blank—
and stand exactly where you sank.

The turnstile clacks its iron tongue.
The announcements praise a train just sprung
from some far platform. No one sees
the small white space between the keys

you never press. The crowd just flows
around the place your body goes—
a stone in river, smoothed and known,
but absolutely, utterly alone.
</div>

### Stone

<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
The river has forgotten how to stop.
No grudge—just the endless drop
and drag of water over head,
a million whispers to the dead

and living both. The stone stays still.
It has no say, it has no will—
just grey and smooth and vaguely round,
the quietest thing for miles around.

The current combs its ancient hair
across the stone's worn cheek. Down there,
no light divides the silt from skin.
The water enters, wears thin,

then leaves again. Each passing drop
believes the journey has to stop
somewhere downstream—but not for long.
The stone remembers being strong

once, sharp once, part of some high ridge
before the rain became a bridge
that washed it here. Now nothing cuts.
The river opens and then shuts

around its back. A fish slides past.
A leaf performs its falling last.
The stone just settles deeper in—
not victory, and not a sin—

just patience wearing down to peace.
The river's fingers never cease
their asking. And the stone gives in—
not lost, not found, just worn so thin

it barely knows where water ends
and where its own slow bruise begins.
The current asks, <em>Are you still there?</em>
The stone replies with only air.
</div>
