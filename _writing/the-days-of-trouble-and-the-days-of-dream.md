*Written in May 2026 for an in-class creative-writing exercise in my Hebrew Bible course. Inspired by [Ecclesiastes 12:1–8](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/ecclesiastes/12), this poem draws on the passage's imagery of aging and mortality while reflecting on the importance of remembering and appreciating life before it passes.*

<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
Before the sun and moon are darkened in,
before the clouds return with rain,
while the grinders still turn thin—
remember, or it will be vain.

The strong men bend,
the doors are shut,
the windows see but dim.
You rise up at a bird.
The rut of time has swallowed him.

The almond tree will bloom—then fall.
The grasshopper drags along.
The silver cord snaps through the hall,
and the silence eats your song.

The pitcher breaks.
The wheel stands still.
The dust goes where it began.
The breath goes back to God,
until the teacher says:
all is vanity, man.

But vanity is not the sum—
the fear, the gray, the fading gleam.
Remember now, before they come:
the days of trouble and the days of dream.
</div>
