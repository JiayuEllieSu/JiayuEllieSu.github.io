*Written in January 2026. Inspired by [“The Snow Man”](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/45235/the-snow-man-56d224a6d4e90) (1921) by Wallace Stevens. The Winter section closely adapts Stevens's poem; Spring, Summer, and Autumn extend its structure and central idea to the other three seasons.*

### Spring

<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
One must have a mind of spring
to notice what begins
without applauding it.
To see the creek unseal
at the thinest edges,
water finding the same low places,
and not call it pardon.
To watch the buds
tighten, then yield,
the green not yet a canopy
but a decision repeated
along each twig.
One must be awake a long time
to smell wet soil
and not name it hope,
to hear rain tap the new leaves
and not imagine a lesson.
The yard is only the yard,
muddy in the shade,
bright in brief openings,
and the watcher, clear of want,
beholds nothing that is not there
and the small, exact arriving that is.
</div>

### Summer

<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
One must have a mind of summer
to sit under the high light
and endure the whole of it.
The heat lays its hand on the porch,
on stone, on skin,
and the air does not explain itself.
The maples hold their thick noise,
the cicadas keep time
without meaning,
and the grass grows as it grows,
not for ease,
not for pleasure.
One must be steady a long time
to feel the day swell
and not call it happiness,
to watch the afternoon slow
and not mistake it for rest.
Even the thunder
is only pressure rearranging,
a darkening, a release.
The world is full
and does not ask consent,
and the listener, quiet in the glare,
hears nothing that is not there
and the blunt, unpitying fullness that is.
</div>

### Autumn

<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
One must have a mind of autumn
to take account
and not perform sadness.
To see the leaves change
as a bookkeeping of light,
green thinning into copper,
then falling, one by one,
not as surrender
but as weight let go.
The fields stand cut,
the seedheads dry,
the trees show their scaffolds.
One must be exact a long time
to smell smoke in the evening
and not call it goodbye,
to hear geese cross overhead
and not appoint them omen.
The season is work:
harvest, clearing,
shortening days,
longer shadows.
And the watcher, freed from elegy,
sees nothing that is not there
and the plain, measured going that is.
</div>

### Winter

<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
One must have a mind of winter
to look at what remains
and not add warmth.
The rail is rimmed with frost.
The branch holds ice.
The sky is a flat, pale sheet.
One must be cold a long time
to hear the wind
and not call it grief,
to hear one leaf scrape and turn
and not call it voice.
The land is bare
and does not offer symbols.
It repeats itself
in the same air, the same drift,
and the listener, nothing himself,
beholds nothing that is not there
and the nothing that is.
</div>
