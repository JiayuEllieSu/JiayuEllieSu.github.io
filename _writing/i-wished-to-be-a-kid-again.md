<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
One day, I looked at myself in the mirror
and saw a little girl.
A girl
who was short,
who was immature,
who was inexperienced.
And so I wished to be an adult,
to grow taller,
to become wiser, steadier,
more mature, more experienced.
I longed for independence,
for the strength to stand on my own,
for the kind of certainty
I thought only adulthood could bring.
But then something came crashing down on me.
The weight of growing up
was heavier than I had imagined.
The world no longer felt simple.
Its edges were sharper,
its expectations louder,
its comforts fewer.
And suddenly,
I found myself wishing for the very thing
I had been so eager to leave behind.
I wished to be a kid again.
To return to a time
when my worries were smaller,
when my heart was lighter,
when being lost did not feel so frightening
because someone was always there
to lead me home.
I wanted the innocence back,
the softness,
the safety of not knowing too much.
But standing there in the mirror,
I realized something painful:
life does not move backward.
The little girl I once was
still lived inside me,
but she could not be called back
to stay.
So now I stand between
what I wanted
and what I miss,
earning that growing up
is not about leaving childhood behind,
but carrying it gently
as you become someone new.
</div>
