<div class="poem" style="white-space: pre-line;">
She pressed it into April soil:
thumb deep, then covered.

The landlord sold the lot in June.
She moved three states away.

The tree forgot her name.

It grows anyway.

Roots split the sidewalk where
she used to sit with coffee.

Children climb it now.
They do not know
who made the shade.

She is not there.
The tree does not miss her.

But the apple that falls
rolls toward a stranger's hand,
and the stranger eats
what hunger prepared
long after the planter left.

The work was not forever.

The work was a thumb in dirt.

Dirt keeps.
Names fade.
Shade lasts without a signature.
